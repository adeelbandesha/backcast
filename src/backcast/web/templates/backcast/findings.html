Findings

Can any of five forecasting models beat the naive forecast on the stocks in the S&P 500? Every member was backtested at three horizons, and so was a placebo copy of each stock on which no model can have a real edge. The placebos show how often the test finds a winner by luck alone.

How the study was run

The universe is the {{ notes.universe.members }} members of the S&P 500 listed on Wikipedia on {{ notes.universe.retrieved }} (the exact revision). Every stock uses five years of daily closes ending {{ study.end }}, and the same backtest as the rest of Backcast: forecasts from each of the last {{ study.origins }} trading days, models re-estimated every {{ study.refit }}, scored at the horizon on prices they had not seen. The method page has the details. {{ study.tested }} of them could be tested.

The placebo replays each stock's own daily moves with their signs drawn at random. The size of every move stays the same, so the volatility, its clustering and the fat tails are all real, but the direction of each day is a coin toss that no model can forecast. One coin toss per date is shared by every stock, so stocks that moved together still move together. That also gives the whole market a single random path, which can trend by chance and hand trend bets easy wins, so the placebo was drawn {{ study.seeds|length }} times, each from its own fixed seed ({{ study.seeds|join:", " }}).

Limits

  • The universe is today's index, so it only holds companies that did well enough to be in it now. Stocks that fell out over the five years are missing, which is survivorship bias. It favours stocks that rose, and so it favours Drift.
  • Stocks move together, so the results for different stocks are not independent and a share of wins can't be given a textbook margin of error. The spread across the {{ study.seeds|length }} placebo draws stands in for one. It is rough: five draws show how far chance moves the share, not its exact limits.
  • Two years of forecasts is one stretch of market history. The next two years could look different.

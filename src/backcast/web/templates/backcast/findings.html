Findings

Every member of the S&P 500 was backtested against the naive forecast at three horizons. Each stock was also run on placebo copies of itself, where no model can have a real edge, to see how often the test finds a winner by luck alone.

What it found

None of the models beat naive by a useful margin. Out of 498 stocks, the SPA test flagged a winner on 8 at the one-day horizon, 2 at five days and 2 at twenty days. The placebos, where any win is luck, gave 1 to 5 winners at one day depending on the draw, 0 to 7 at five days and 2 to 20 at twenty days. All of this uses closes up to {{ study.end }}.

So at five and twenty days the real stocks did no better than the placebos. At one day, 8 is more than any placebo draw gave, but the wins are tiny. On those 8 stocks the best model's error was only 0.1% to 0.5% lower than naive's.

The test was stricter than its 0.05 cutoff suggests. On the placebos it flagged 0.4% to 1.7% of stocks on average, not the 5% you might expect, so the real results are compared with the placebos and not with 5%.

Seven of the eight one-day wins went to Theta or ETS, and they come from small reversals. Over these two years, a stock's daily move tended to be partly undone the next day. The median correlation between one day's return and the next was −0.024, and it was negative for 70% of stocks. Theta and ETS forecasts sit a little behind the latest price, so they gain when a move partly reverses. Theta's error was lower than naive's on 59% of the real stocks but only 33% of the placebos, where the random signs wipe out any reversal. At the median stock the difference was 0.02%, far too small to trade on.

Valero is a good example of a trend passing for skill. It won at one day and at twenty, both times with Drift, which assumes the recent trend continues, and its price roughly tripled over the period. At twenty days Drift's error was 11% lower than naive's when the price was above its {{ trend_days }}-day average, and 2% higher when it was below. The other twenty-day winner, Phillips 66, is also an oil refiner.

LightGBM, the only machine-learning model, came last. At the median stock its error was 2% to 3% higher than naive's, and the Diebold-Mariano test didn't put it ahead of naive on a single stock at any horizon. Drift should have been helped by survivorship bias, since every stock here made it into the 2026 index, but the same test put it ahead on just one stock.

How the study was run

The universe is the {{ notes.universe.members }} members of the S&P 500 listed on Wikipedia on {{ notes.universe.retrieved }} (this revision). {{ study.tested }} of them were tested at one horizon or more; the first table counts each horizon. Every stock gets five years of daily closes ending {{ study.end }} and the same backtest as the rest of Backcast: a forecast from each of the last {{ study.origins }} trading days, models re-estimated every {{ study.refit }}, scored on prices they had not seen. The method page has the details.

A placebo replays a stock's own daily moves with random signs. Each move keeps its size, so the placebo is just as volatile as the real stock, with the same calm and wild stretches. Only the direction is random, and no model can forecast a coin toss. Each date gets one coin toss for all stocks, so stocks that moved together still do.

That also gives the whole market a single random path. If that path happens to trend, trend bets win on many placebos at once, so a single draw could make luck look more or less common than it is. The placebo was drawn {{ study.seeds|length|apnumber }} times, from the seeds {{ study.seeds|join:", " }}.

Limits

  • The universe is today's index. Companies that fell out of it over the five years are missing, and the ones left did well enough to stay. This survivorship bias favours stocks that rose, and with them Drift.
  • Stocks move together, so their results are not independent, and a share of wins has no textbook margin of error. The spread across the placebo draws is the stand-in. With this few draws it shows roughly how far chance moves the share, not its exact limits.
  • These are two particular years of forecasts, and the next two could go differently.
